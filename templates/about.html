About this project

The problem, the data, how the models work and what they cannot do.

Why it is called Delta

Δ is the maths symbol for difference, and every screen here is about one: the brochure mileage against the real-road mileage, one model's prediction against another's, the training score against the test score, city riding against highway riding, and one fuel against another. The logo shows it too. A solid triangle is the number you are told, and the coral sliver beside it is the gap.

The problem

Brochure mileage is rarely what you get on the road, and fuel prices change often. Most people cannot easily tell what a trip will cost. Delta predicts a vehicle's mileage from its specifications and turns it into a rupee cost for the distance and fuel price you enter.

The data

Three real datasets are used, one per vehicle type. Two of the files you might expect to use were left out on purpose.

FileWhat it became
Bike_Features.csvBikes and scooters: {{ metrics.categories.bike.rows }} models after removing colour variants. Specs and mileage were parsed out of the file, because many rows have shifted columns.

bike_sales_india.csvNot used. Engine size, fuel and mileage are unrelated to the model name (the Bajaj CT 100 appears with engines from 109 to 996 cc, and "Electric" bikes have a km/l figure), so it looks randomly generated.
CAR DETAILS FROM CAR DEKHO.csvNot used. It has prices and kilometres driven, but no mileage or engine data.

The pipeline

  • Split the rows 80% to learn and 20% to test.
  • Fill gaps with the median, scale numbers and one-hot encode brand and type.
  • Train six regression models for mileage and six classification models for the efficiency class.
  • Score on the test rows and with 5-fold cross-validation. Permutation importance shows which inputs matter.

From rated mileage to your trip cost

The datasets list rated mileage, so that is what the models predict. Real roads differ. The multipliers below are assumptions, not learned from data. You can change them in features.py.

  • Road: city ×{{ road.city }}, mixed ×{{ road.mixed }}, highway ×{{ road.highway }}
  • Extra weight lowers mileage by {{ (cars.car.load_penalty * 100)|round|int }}% per 100 kg for cars and {{ (cars.bike.load_penalty * 100)|round|int }}% for bikes
  • Age: {{ (age_penalty * 1000)|round / 10 }}% per year, up to 15 years. Overdue service ×{{ service }}. Car air conditioning ×{{ ac }}
  • Cars run on petrol in the data. Diesel is ×{{ fuel_factor.diesel }} and CNG ×{{ fuel_factor.cng }}, as estimates.

Trip cost = distance ÷ real-road mileage × the fuel price you type.

Limits

  • The car data is from 1970–82 vehicles. Modern cars are usually more efficient than the numbers shown.
  • Bike mileage is the listed figure, not measured on a road.
  • The electric scooter set is small (42 models), and these are kick-scooter style vehicles. Treat those predictions as rough.
  • Fuel prices are never fetched online. You enter them, so the cost is only as current as the price you type.

Future scope

  • Add newer car data with Indian models and CNG variants.
  • Learn the road and load effects from logged trips instead of assuming them.
  • Fetch fuel prices by city.
